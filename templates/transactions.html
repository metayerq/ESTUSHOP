What this page cannot tell you — and how it measures
Regulars are not measurable, and no proxy will do
The till is anonymous: client.fiscal_id reads '---------' on 59 of 60 tickets sampled since opening. No substitute — ticket size, weekday habits, hourly patterns — would be that measurement; it would only look like one.
People: counted at the table, estimated at the counter
Mesa asks how many people sit down and writes it on the document. The rest is estimated from drinks: 1 drink = 1 person, floor 1, ceiling 8. The share actually counted is shown next to the figure it concerns, never here — a provenance that lives at the bottom of a page is a provenance nobody reads.
June is excluded, and so is the opening
The analysis starts at analysis_start, not opening_day. The café opened 27 May 2026; the weeks that follow carry the run-in and group orders of up to 55 drinks, which would move every median here. So this page cannot compare today against the opening period at all.
No reference August exists
This is the café's first summer. Nothing here tells an August dip from a decline, or a holiday lull from a lost habit. That comparison becomes possible in 2027, not before.
No trend, on purpose
Nothing is smoothed, fitted or extended: every line that looks like a line is a step of measured window medians, and every comparison is between two named windows with their n. A regression on this few days would draw the noise with the authority of a straight line.
